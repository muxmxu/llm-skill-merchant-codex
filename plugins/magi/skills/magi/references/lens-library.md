# Lens library

Candidate seats for a MAGI council. Not a menu to be used whole — pick the
few the proposition actually turns on. Each entry gives what the seat asks,
what evidence moves it, and when it earns a seat.

## Orthogonality test

Before seating a lens, check it against the ones already seated:

1. Would one piece of evidence move both seats in the same direction at the
   same time? If yes, they are one seat. Keep the sharper one.
2. Can you name a fact that would make this seat vote against while another
   seat votes for? If you cannot, the seat adds a vote but no information.
3. Does this seat have a source of evidence the others do not read — a
   different log, repository, deadline calendar, paper, or reviewer's words?

A council of three orthogonal seats is worth more than five overlapping ones.

## Evidence load-bearing

*Asks*: does the evidence this decision rests on support what it is being
asked to support? Which claim breaks if it does not?

*Moved by*: the original log or run output rather than a summary; sample sizes;
whether the number quoted is the number measured.

*Seat it when*: the proposition is justified by a prior result, or the proposed
work is meant to produce evidence for a claim already written down.

## Internal validity and confounds

*Asks*: would the proposed work distinguish the hypothesis from its
alternatives, or would the result fit several explanations?

*Moved by*: controls, held-fixed variables, covariation, prior null results,
and what those results failed to rule out.

*Seat it when*: the decision is whether to run an experiment or probe.

## Cost and schedule

*Asks*: what does this consume — compute, person-hours, calendar — and what
does the deadline permit once that is subtracted?

*Moved by*: measured durations of comparable runs, queue and hardware
constraints, and dates that cannot move.

*Seat it when*: almost always.

## Opportunity cost and alternatives

*Asks*: what does this displace, and is there a cheaper route to the same
decision-relevant information?

*Moved by*: the queued work and cheaper probes that resolve the same uncertainty.

*Seat it when*: resources are contended or multiple routes address one claim.

## Outside evaluation

*Asks*: how will a reviewer, advisor, or reader receive this? Where will it be
attacked?

*Moved by*: actual reviewer comments, venue criteria, and comparable published
work.

*Seat it when*: the decision affects a submission, talk, or report outside the
group.

## Risk and reversibility

*Asks*: what is the worst outcome, and can it be undone? Does it consume a
submission slot, subject pool, budget, or collaborator goodwill?

*Moved by*: whether an undo path exists and has been exercised.

*Seat it when*: the action is one-way or touches money, people, or an external
party.

## Existing evidence: already answered?

*Asks*: has the question already been answered in an old log, prior run, or
paper and merely forgotten?

*Moved by*: the record itself. This seat retrieves; it does not speculate.

*Seat it when*: the work has a long history and the proposal sounds familiar.

## Data and statistical power

*Asks*: at the proposed scale, could the result distinguish the outcomes it
needs to distinguish? What effect size would be detectable?

*Moved by*: prior variance, number of conditions, and the planned comparison.

*Seat it when*: the proposal is a measurement or evaluation and scale is
negotiable.

## Ethics, compliance, and people

*Asks*: who is affected, what did they agree to, and what does the institution
require?

*Moved by*: consent and recruitment terms, institutional rules, and what was
promised to participants or collaborators.

*Seat it when*: human subjects, personal data, paid participants, or another
party's material is involved. A genuine ethical blocker is not outvoted.
